Edward Lorenz’s famous butterfly is the 1963 convection model. Lorenz 86 is a later low-order atmosphere system: five knobs, a compact folded cloud, and no twin lobes. Same name on the label, different ODEs.

Classical Chaos draws it like the other 3D stages: Euler steps into a GPU line trail. The embed below only grows the trail in. Scrubbing length, drag-to-rotate, and the coefficients live on the full canvas. Classic Lorenz stays at `/lorenz_attractor`.

## The ODEs

Four real coefficients plus step size. On the Params panel they are `a`, `b`, `f`, `g`, and `d` (the Euler step).

<BlockMath math={'\\dot{x} = -a x - y^2 - z^2 + a b'} />
<BlockMath math={'\\dot{y} = -y + x y - f x z + g'} />
<BlockMath math={'\\dot{z} = -z + f x y + x z'} />

Defaults match the [archive workshop](https://github.com/kgz/CanvasWorld_archive/tree/master/%283d%29%20Lorenz%2086): `a=1.111`, `b=4.494`, `f=1.479`, `g=0.44`, `d=0.13`, seed `(0, 0, 0)`. The constant drive terms (`a b` in \(x\), `g` in \(y\)) push the state off the origin immediately.

## Why it is not the butterfly

The 1963 system stretches and folds into two lobes. Here the quadratic terms in \(x\) and the bilinear coupling through \(f\) keep a single tangled cloud. Nudge `f` and the twist changes. Nudge `a`/`b` and the overall scale of the drive shifts. Nothing is a mesh; it is only the orbit you integrate.

## Trail, not a wrap buffer

The old workshop wrote points into a ring buffer and wrapped. Here `n` is trail length: grow, scrub, or hold, same contract as Lorenz and Aizawa. Change any of `a`, `b`, `f`, `g`, or `d` and the trail resets from the seed.

```ts
const next = lorenz86Tick(x, y, z, a, b, f, g, d)
// archive rotated the cloud by π/2 about z
```
