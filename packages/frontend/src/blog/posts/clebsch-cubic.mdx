Smooth cubic surfaces over the complex numbers always carry 27 lines. On a random real cubic, most of those lines hide in complex space. Clebsch’s diagonal cubic (1871) is the famous exception: every one of the 27 is real, and ten points (Eckardt points) are where three lines meet at once.

Classical Chaos marches the Hunt / Nordstrand affine equation to a lit triangle mesh. Color follows the sample box; the surface grows in as transport <code>n</code> reveals more triangles.

## The affine field

In ordinary <InlineMath math={'x,y,z'} /> the zero set is

<BlockMath
	math={
		'81(x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3})-189(x^{2}y+x^{2}z+xy^{2}+xz^{2}+y^{2}z+yz^{2})+54xyz+126(xy+xz+yz)-9(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})-9(x+y+z)+1=0'
	}
/>

That polynomial is symmetric under permuting <InlineMath math={'x,y,z'} />, which is why the mesh has a three-fold look even before you spin it.

## Same surface, projective form

Dropping to a hyperplane in <InlineMath math={'\\mathbb{P}^{4}'} /> recovers the cleaner pair

<BlockMath math={'x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=0'} />

<BlockMath math={'x_{0}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+x_{4}^{3}=0'} />

The affine chart above is what the mesh actually samples. The 27 lines live on the projective surface; this page does not draw them as overlays. You get the cubic shell itself.

## How the mesh gets there

Sample the field on a regular 3D grid, keep the zero isosurface as triangles, then light more index range over time so the shell fills in. On the [full Clebsch stage](/clebsch_cubic) there are no extra knobs: scrub how much of the mesh is lit and drag to rotate.
