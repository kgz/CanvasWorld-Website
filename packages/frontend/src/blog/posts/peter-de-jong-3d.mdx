The planar Peter de Jong map needs four numbers. Add a third coordinate and two more knobs and you get a different object: a discrete orbit that fills a volume instead of a sheet.

Classical Chaos ports the archive 3D workshop as a progressive GPU line trail (same scrub contract as Lorenz), not a wrap buffer of points. Watch it grow in below.

## The update rule

Each step replaces `(x, y, z)` together from the previous state. No continuous `dt`. The map:

<BlockMath math={'x\\prime = \\sin(a z) - \\cos(b x)'} />
<BlockMath math={'y\\prime = \\sin(c x) - \\cos(d y)'} />
<BlockMath math={'z\\prime = \\sin(e y) - \\cos(f z)'} />

Six real knobs on the Params panel: `a` through `f`. The planar de Jong cousin stops at four knobs and a flat cloud. Here a third sine–cosine pair feeds `z`, so the orbit fills a volume.

## Archive defaults

On Classical Chaos the defaults match the [archive 3D sketch](https://github.com/kgz/CanvasWorld_archive/tree/master/%283d%29%20Peter%20Dejong%20attractor): `a = 2.695`, `b = 1.72`, `c = 1.178`, `d = 0.311`, `e = -1`, `f = -1`, seed `(0, 0, 0)`, positions scaled ×50. Presets swap in other `(a…f)` tuples in `[-5, 5]`.

## Trail length, not a ring buffer

The old workshop wrote into a fixed particle array and wrapped when full. Here `n` is how much of the saved orbit is lit. Grow, scrub, or hold. Change any of `a`…`f` and the trail resets from the seed.

```ts
const next = peterDeJong3dTick(x, y, z, a, b, c, d, e, f)
// store (x, y, z) * 50
```
